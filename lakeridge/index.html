Lakeridge Community Credit Union: ITGC engagement

IT general controls testing in support of the FY2026 financial statement audit of an Ontario credit union whose core banking system is run by a service provider.

Entity

Ontario credit union, FSRA regulated. $2.1B assets, 48,000 members, 14 branches, 240 staff.

Year end

October 31, 2026. IFRS.

The complication

Core banking hosted and operated by a service provider. The client does not run its own system of record.

My role

IT audit team member supporting the external audit. Scoping to deficiency evaluation.

The organization, service providers (Northline, Keystone, Lakeshore Central, Ledgerline), evidence and results are fictional, built to practise the work. No client data is shown.

Approach

Four deliverables, one line of reasoning

1 · Scope

Start from what the audit relies on

Set materiality ($550K, 5% of pre-tax income). Identified the automated controls, reports and IT-dependent manual controls the audit planned to use. Scoped applications from that list, not from the systems inventory. Every exclusion names the control that covers the risk instead.

IT-100 Scoping memo

2 · Service organization

Read the SOC 1 like a user auditor

Evaluated the provider's SOC 1 Type II under CAS 402: opinion, period, exceptions, carve-outs. Mapped all 19 CUECs to control objectives and looked for the client control behind each. Asked the provider whether two unnamed exceptions were the client's. Both were.

IT-200 SOC 1 and CUEC analysis

3 · Test

Four ITGC domains

Walkthroughs for design. Samples or full populations for operating effectiveness, with completeness proven first. Where design failed, impact assessments instead of samples. Reports behind manual controls tested directly.

IT-320 Change management workpaper

4 · Evaluate

CAS 265, individually and together

Weighed likelihood, magnitude, fraud risk and compensating controls for 17 deficiencies. Aggregated them by root cause into three significant deficiencies for the Audit Committee, with draft wording.

IT-900 Deficiency evaluation

What the work found

Three findings that changed the audit

The provider's controls worked. The client's half did not exist.

The SOC 1 opinion was unmodified. But the client had no process for most of its 19 CUECs. Nobody reviewed access, approved configuration changes or read the nightly exception reports. The report had been filed without being read, and the Board was told it was "on file." The access, change and batch objectives were not achieved for this client, whatever the opinion said. The audit went substantive for interest, balances and system reports.

Testing completeness in reverse found changes nobody logged.

Sampling from the change log only covers changes someone chose to log. So I also started from system audit logs and traced back to tickets. Infrastructure traced cleanly. Four of ten financial reporting configuration changes had no ticket at all.

A compensating control that covered accuracy, not classification.

Two finance staff could make and approve their own mapping changes. Management offered a monthly reconciliation as a compensating control. It worked, but it compares balances, so it cannot see a correct balance in the wrong line. The year-end mapping review found a $1.9M account misclassified after a February change. A misstatement found by the auditor, not the client, made this a significant deficiency.

Judgment calls

Decisions I would defend in review

The gap period was not the deciding factor

The SOC report ended a month before year end. Bridge letter and roll-forward were planned, but reliance had already failed for the whole year because of the client's CUECs. Knowing which problem drives the conclusion matters.

No sampling out of exceptions

Ten of 25 change approvals were not evidenced. The expectation for an ITGC is zero. I found the root cause (no cover for the change board secretary for five months) and concluded the control did not operate.

No misstatement does not mean an effective control

146 self-approved corrections to member accounts, all eventually supported. The impact result informs likelihood. The segregation failure is still a significant deficiency because of fraud risk.

Close calls written down

Two deficiencies could reasonably go either way. The memo records both sides so a reviewer can challenge the reasoning, not just the answer.
