SME controls and compliance review: Port Alder Community Church

An advisory review of how a Ontario church of about 480 people receives, records, receipts and protects donations, after an email impersonation cost it $1,500 in gift cards. First contact to the ninety-day follow-up.

Entity

Registered charity, ONCA public benefit corporation. $1.18M revenue, 94% donations. Four staff, one part-time bookkeeper.

Five ways to give

Plate offerings, a church-built website giving page, a recurring giving platform, a foyer card terminal and Interac e-Transfer.

The complication

Nobody owned the website. The domain, DNS and hosting sat in a former volunteer's personal accounts.

My role

Independent advisor. Findings and recommendations, not assurance. No CAS or CSAE opinion.

The church, the town of Port Alder, every person and vendor (Lakeport Payments, TithePath, GraceGive, MapleNest, Shepherd Base) and all evidence are fictional, built to practise the work. No client data is shown.

Approach

Scoped to what a small charity actually needs

A church has no ICFR regime and is not a reporting issuer. Its auditor forms an opinion on the financial statements and does not look at the website, card obligations, receipting or access. This review sits beside that audit, never inside it.

1 · Before the first call

Strictly passive reconnaissance

Viewed the public giving page as a donor would and read public DNS, registration and certificate records. Found card fields on the church's own page, 14 scripts beside them, broken SPF and DMARC at p=none. No logins, no form submissions, no scanning. The boundary is written into the engagement letter.

2 · Contract

Advisory, and said plainly

Priced from hours by area ($8,400 for 74 hours at a charity rate), not from the client's approval limit. The engagement letter sets what I may and may not touch, and a stop-and-report rule for fraud indicators and card data. Both rules were used.

3 · Fieldwork

Follow the money through every door

Observed a Sunday count. Traced a $50 gift from click to bank to giving records to receipt, and a $1 test donation from my own card through the browser. Tested 12 Sundays three ways, all 27 refunds, ten receipts against Income Tax Regulations s. 3501, and 64 volunteers' screening.

4 · Report

Written for a board with no technical member

Sixteen findings with condition, criteria, cause, effect and recommendation. One Critical, seven High. Management's disagreement recorded beside the finding. Eight questions the board now asks every quarter.

What the work found

Three findings that changed the engagement

The page only looked like SAQ A-EP.

A browser trace showed card data going straight to the processor, the SAQ A-EP pattern. The server review showed the plugin had a fallback route that sent cards to the church's own server, and a support-enabled log that had kept 214 of them, with security codes, in a public folder for 19 months. I stopped the volunteer who reached to delete the files, contained them in 24 minutes, and told the chair within the hour. The treasurer, not me, reported to the processor.

Five refunds to one card, and nothing more said.

One shared login could send refunds to any card with no approval. Twenty-two of 27 refunds were supported. Five, all to one card linked to no donor, were not. Under the engagement letter I stopped, reported to the chair, and recommended counsel. When the room offered names, I confirmed none. The report gives the count, the total and the fact of referral.

Matching deposits proved nothing about the plate.

All twelve tested deposits matched their count sheets to the cent. The treasurer argued that justified a lower rating. It doesn't: a loss before the count would produce exactly that result. The rating stayed High, and his view went into the report in his own words.

Judgment calls

Decisions I would defend in review

I corrected my own memo in the first line

The interim memo led with "SAQ A-EP" and put the open server check in its last section. When the check changed the answer, the correction said so in its first sentence and apologised once. The lesson: a condition belongs in the headline. MEMO-02

A rating moved on evidence, not pressure

Email impersonation was proposed High. Management brought SPF fixed, DKIM on, DMARC at quarantine and a signed call-back rule. Likelihood changed, so it went to Medium. The offering count rating was pushed just as hard, with no new evidence, and stayed High.

PIPEDA as a benchmark, not a charge

PIPEDA reaches only a charity's commercial activities. The report measures donor, prayer and screening records against its fair information principles as good practice, and refers the applicability question to counsel rather than asserting non-compliance.

Design the obligation away

A volunteer-run church cannot operate script inventories and change detection. The recommendation was the processor's hosted payment page, which moves the church to the shortest questionnaire. Card giving returned that way in February.

Declined part of the retainer

The board asked me to run the website and help prepare the quarterly report. I declined both: I would then be checking my own work. The retainer covers twice-yearly review only.

Priced from the work, not the approval limit

The committee could approve $5,000 alone. Pricing at $4,950 was tempting. The real objection turned out to be this year's budget, so splitting payment across two fiscal years won the vote with no rate cut.
