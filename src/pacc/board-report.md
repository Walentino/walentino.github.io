**About this report.** This report comes from an **advisory engagement**. It sets out what I found and what I recommend. It is **not an audit**, a review engagement, or any other kind of assurance engagement. I am not a public accounting firm, and nothing here is an opinion on the church's financial statements, its internal controls, its compliance with any law or standard, or the security of its systems. The report does not say the church is safe or compliant, and must not be quoted in donor communications or described as an audit or certification. Two matters in section 8 are still open, and this report expresses no view on either.

## 1. Summary for the board

### The question you asked

In September 2026 someone impersonated the pastor by email, and a volunteer bought $1,500 in gift cards. In October someone impersonated a supplier and nearly redirected a payment. The board asked whether these were isolated events, or signs that money or information could go wrong at the church without anyone noticing.

### The answer

**They were not isolated.** In almost every area I looked at, the church depends on trusted people doing the right thing. Very little would show the board if someone made a mistake, or did something wrong. That is common in churches of this size, and it is fixable. But it is the main thing this report asks the board to change.

### The three things that matter most

1. **Donors' card details were stored on the church's website server.** From April 2025 to December 2026, a setting in the donation software recorded the full card details of 214 failed donation attempts. This was found on December 8, contained within half an hour, and reported to the card processor the same day. Whether anyone accessed the files earlier cannot be confirmed or ruled out. *(F1, Critical)*
2. **The church does not control its own name on the internet.** The web address, its settings and the hosting are all held personally by a former volunteer. The web address expires on March 14, 2027. If it lapses, the website, online giving and every church email address stop working. *(F3, High)*
3. **Money can leave without anyone noticing or being able to say who sent it.** Everyone in the office shares one login to the card processor, which allows refunds to any card with no approval. No one compares what is counted on Sunday, what reaches the bank and what is recorded for receipts. *(F4, F5, F6, High)*

## 3. Money coming in

### F5. Sunday offerings: custody and counting · High

**What I found.** After the service, plates are emptied into an unsealed cloth bag that one person carries to the counting room. On the Sunday I observed, that person was about fifteen. The two counters each count a different part of the offering, and neither checks the other's. On that Sunday, and on two of the twelve Sundays I tested, one counter finished the count alone. Cheques are not stamped "for deposit only". Seven people know the safe code, which has not changed since 2019.

**Why it matters.** Every other way people give leaves a record. Cash doesn't. Once the count is done, the count sheet is the only evidence of what was in the plate. If cash went missing before or during the count, nothing would show it, and nothing would protect the counters from suspicion if a question were raised.

**What the testing showed.** For all twelve Sundays tested, the bank deposit matched the count sheet to the cent. That shows the counted money reached the bank. It cannot show what was in the plate before it was counted, which is the gap this finding is about.

**What I recommend.** A short written count procedure. Tamper-evident numbered bags, sealed at the back of the sanctuary and carried by two adults. Both counters count all the cash and sign only once totals are written. If one counter must leave, the count stops and the bag is sealed. A "for deposit only" stamp. Safe code changed now and known by no more than three named people.

**Treasurer's view.** *"In nine years there has been no loss, and the deposits match the count every time. I believe this should be rated Medium. I accept the recommendations."*

**Why I kept it at High.** Matching deposits are exactly what a loss before the count would look like. The rating reflects the absence of anything that would detect a loss, not any suggestion that one occurred.

### F6. No check that counts, bank deposits and giving records agree · High

**What I found.** Three records describe the same money: what was counted or charged, what reached the bank, and what was entered in the giving system for donors' receipts. Nobody compares the third with the other two. In three of twelve Sundays tested, the giving system did not match the count. One $250 gift was never entered, so that donor would not have received a receipt for it. Building Fund gifts reach the accounts only through a monthly journal from a report nobody checks.

**What I recommend.** A monthly one-page reconciliation of giving system totals by fund against the bank and the processors, prepared by the bookkeeper and initialled by the treasurer.

### F4. Refunds: one shared login, no approval, refunds to any card · High

**What I found.** Everyone in the office signs in to the card processor's portal with the same login. Any refund, of any amount, can be issued without approval, and can be sent to a card other than the one the donor used. No one reviews the list of refunds. I examined every refund and credit from January 2025 to November 2026, 27 in total. Twenty-two had a clear reason and supporting records. **Five did not, and have been referred to the board chair under section 7.1 of the engagement letter.** See section 8.

**What I recommend.** A personal login for each person who needs the portal. Ask the processor to switch off refunds to other cards. A monthly refund report reviewed by someone who does not issue refunds.

### F15. The September loss paid from the benevolence fund; insurer not told · Low

The $1,500 reimbursed to the volunteer was charged to the benevolence fund, which holds gifts given to help people in need. The insurer was not told. Move the charge to general expenses, and report any future loss to the insurer within days.

## 4. Card giving and the website

*A note on terms. Any organisation that accepts cards agrees, through its card processor, to follow the card industry's security standard, called PCI DSS, and confirms each year that it does so, usually by completing a self-assessment questionnaire. The more card information touches the organisation's own systems, the longer and harder the questionnaire.*

### F1. Donors' card details stored on the website server · Critical

**What I found.** The website's donation software has a backup route it uses when the processor's own software fails to load in a donor's browser. On that route, card details are sent to the church's server. Since April 29, 2025, a setting, apparently switched on during the software company's support work, saved every failed attempt to a file on the server, including full card number, expiry date and three-digit security code. There were 214 entries. The files were in a folder reachable from the internet by anyone who knew the address.

**What happened next.** Found on December 8. Within thirty minutes the setting was off and public access blocked, without deleting anything. By early afternoon the card form had been removed from the website, the insurer notified, a lawyer engaged, and the processor told by the treasurer. The web host's records for the previous fourteen days show no outside access. No earlier records exist.

**What I recommend.** Follow the processor's instructions in full and keep the files until the processor and counsel say otherwise. Do not return card giving to the website until F2's recommendation is in place. Follow counsel's advice on telling donors.

### F2. Website card giving under the strictest rules, without the required protections · High

**What I found.** Because card details could reach the church's server, the website fell under the longest questionnaire, SAQ D. Before December 8 I had concluded, from browser testing, that it fell under a shorter one, SAQ A-EP. The server review showed that was wrong, and I corrected it in writing on December 9. Under either version, the required protections were missing: current software, control of outside software on the donation page, detection of changes to that page, quarterly external scans, and an agreement with the web host. The church had paid about $1,100 in monthly fees since 2023 for never filing a self-assessment. Staff had also taken card numbers by phone and written some in a notebook, now destroyed.

**What I recommend.** When card giving returns to the website, use the processor's own hosted payment page, so donors type card details on the processor's page, not the church's. That reduces the church's obligations to the shortest questionnaire, and filing it ends the monthly fee.

### F3. Web address, settings and hosting held by a former volunteer · High

**What I found.** The web address was registered in 2014 by a youth volunteer, who still holds it, the DNS settings and the hosting in his own name. He moved out of province in 2024. The registration account could not be opened because its recovery email no longer exists, and automatic renewal relied on an expired card. The address expires March 14, 2027. The former volunteer has been helpful and committed to dates for each transfer.

**What I recommend.** Complete the transfers, put renewal dates in the treasurer's calendar, and give every critical account two named administrators, at least one paid.

### F13. Foyer card machine · Medium

The card machine stays unattended on the welcome desk, with its refund password handwritten on a label underneath. Nobody checks it for tampering. Remove the label and change the password, store the machine in the office between services, and do a quick visual check before each service.

## 5. Systems, email and backups

### F7. Shared administrator accounts; no process when people leave · High

The most powerful Microsoft 365 account, which can read anyone's email, was shared, had no second sign-in check, and its password was on a card in the safe. The same password opened a shared website administrator account. Across six systems I found former volunteers, a former treasurer and a software company's support account still able to sign in. One volunteer has been the only administrator of the giving system since 2015. There is no leaver checklist. Most of this was fixed during the review. A leaver checklist and an annual access review by the treasurer remain.

### F8. Church email could be forged; payment change checks informal · Medium

The settings that let other email systems confirm a message came from the church were broken or missing. That is how, in September, an email could show the pastor's real address without coming from the church. Sign-in records show his account was not accessed. Settings were corrected and tightened during the review, and all staff signed a rule that any bank change or gift card request is confirmed by phone. *Proposed as High; lowered to Medium on the evidence of these changes.*

### F12. The church holds no copy of its own records · Medium

Microsoft 365 backup works: a restore was demonstrated on December 9. The giving and accounting systems are backed up only by their providers, and the church has never exported its own copy. Export both each quarter to restricted church storage.

## 6. Receipts, filings and personal information

### F9. Tax receipts · Medium

All ten 2025 receipts tested lacked the place of issue and showed an outdated CRA web address. The fund used for paid events is set to issue receipts, so retreat attendees who paid $140 were receipted for $140, although about $112 paid for their meals and lodging: $4,368 receipted across 39 attendees that should not have been. When a donor receives something in return, only the amount above its value can be receipted. Correct the template and fund setting before February, and get advice from the auditor or a charity tax adviser on the 2026 retreat and the 2025 receipts already issued.

### F16. 2024 charity return filed late · Low

The 2024 T3010 was filed nine days after its June 30 due date. Put the deadline in two calendars.

### F10. Children's ministry screening · High

Of 64 people on the fall rosters, 61 had current vulnerable sector checks. Three had lapsed and were still serving. Paper records are well kept. But scans had been forwarded to a personal email account and saved in a folder shared with nine people, and records go back to 2014. No one should serve without a current check; track expiry dates with alerts; keep copies in one restricted folder; agree a retention period with counsel. The three volunteers stepped back the day this was discussed.

### F11. Privacy notice · Medium

The 2021 privacy notice says the church never shares information. Advertising and analytics tools run on every page, including the prayer request and children's sign-up pages, and the giving system stores data in the United States. Remove the tools from sensitive pages and update the notice. Canada's federal privacy law applies only to some of a charity's activities, but its principles remain the recognised standard, and donors and parents expect the church to keep its word.

## 7. Board oversight

### F14. The board receives too little to spot a problem · Medium

The board receives audited statements once a year and budget comparisons. None of the information that would have shown the problems in this report reaches it. With so few staff, nobody can check everyone else's work, so the board's regular review becomes one of the church's most important controls.

### The quarterly report: eight questions

| # | Question | Who answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Did the monthly reconciliations of giving records to the bank and processors agree, and if not, why not? | Bookkeeper |
| 2 | What refunds were issued, by whom, to which card, and who reviewed them? | Treasurer |
| 3 | Were any Sunday counts done by fewer than two people? | Treasurer |
| 4 | Who joined or left roles with system access, and was access added or removed? | Office administrator |
| 5 | Are all children's ministry volunteers' checks current? | Safeguarding lead |
| 6 | Were any requests to change bank details or buy gift cards received, and how were they confirmed? | Treasurer |
| 7 | When does each critical account next renew, and is it in a named person's calendar? | Treasurer |
| 8 | What is the status of each open finding in this report? | Treasurer |

## 9. What is working well

- **Counted cash reaches the bank intact.** All twelve Sundays tested matched to the cent.
- **The treasurer refused a fake e-Transfer "return" request** in April 2026 after checking the bank.
- **A supplier call-back stopped a fraud** in October. It is now a written rule.
- **Screening is taken seriously.** Ninety-five percent of volunteers were current, and lapsed volunteers were stood down the same day.
- **The church responded to the most serious finding well.** Contained, preserved, and reported to the processor and insurer within four hours.
- **Staff engaged openly.** Where records didn't exist, staff said so rather than creating them.

## 10. Scope, approach and limitations

**In scope:** offerings and deposits; website, recurring, phone, e-Transfer and terminal giving, refunds and the providers involved; domain, DNS and hosting; receipting and the T3010; email security and payment fraud; access to main systems; personal information including screening records; backup; board oversight. Activity from January 1, 2025.

**How:** interviews and walkthroughs; observation of one Sunday count; systems viewed on screen with staff operating them, never using anyone's password; inspection of records and exports; tests of samples or full populations; one $1 donation from my own card with written approval, refunded the same day. Before the engagement, public information only.

**Not done:** no audit or assurance of any kind; no scanning, penetration testing or attempts to bypass controls; no investigation of any person; no legal or tax advice; no testing of providers' own controls.

**Limitations:** testing was largely sample-based; controls can be overridden by collusion or by people in authority; conditions change after fieldwork. Server access was not available until December 8, and an interim memo of December 4 that relied on browser testing alone was corrected in writing on December 9. Finding F2 reflects the corrected position.

**Use:** for the board and management. May be shared whole with the church's auditor, insurer, counsel and payment processor with the statement: *"This report was produced by an advisory engagement and does not provide assurance."*
